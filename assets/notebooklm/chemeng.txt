Flow Measurement
Flow measurement is the determination of the quantity of fluid
passing through a given section per unit time.
Why is it important?
• Process control — maintain the required flow of raw materials and products
• Material balance — determine input, output and consumption
• Energy management — monitor steam, cooling water and fuel flow
• Safety — prevent excessive or insufficient flow
• Product quality — maintain accurate process conditions
• Equipment operation — pumps, heat exchangers and reactors require
specified flow rates

ROTAMETER
Working Principle
• Fluid enters from the bottom and flows upward through the tapered
tube.
• The upward flow produces a drag force on the float.
• The float rises until the upward forces balance its effective weight.
At equilibrium:
FD+FB=W
where FD is drag force, FB is buoyancy force, and W is the weight of the
float.
Flow Measurement
• As the float rises, the available annular flow area around the float
increases.
• A higher flow rate causes the float to reach a higher equilibrium
position.
• The float position is calibrated directly in terms of flow rate.
Higher flow rate→Higher float position

2. Flow rate with a venturimeter and an orificemeter (Chemical
Engineering)
Aim: determine the flow rate of water in a pipe using a venturimeter and an orificemeter.
Venturimeter: inlet (pipe diameter) → convergent cone → cylindrical throat → gradually divergent

cone back to pipe size. Pressure taps at the inlet and the throat. Gradual shape → small losses → C_d ≈
0.95–0.98.
Orificemeter: a thin flat plate with a concentric hole. The jet keeps converging just downstream to the

vena contracta (minimum area, maximum velocity, minimum pressure), then expands with large losses →
C_d ≈ 0.6–0.65, cheaper and compact.

Principle: continuity (A₁V₁ = A₂V₂) + Bernoulli → the narrower section has higher velocity and lower
pressure; the manometer measures the difference.

3. Build your own pH scale (Chemistry)
Aim: make a simple pH scale using a natural indicator from turmeric. Hypothesis: plant pigments change
colour with acidity, so household substances can be ranked by pH.
pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; acids < 7 < bases (at 25 °C); one pH unit = a factor of 10 in [H⁺].
Turmeric contains curcumin. In acidic to neutral solutions it stays protonated and yellow. In alkaline
solutions phenolic deprotonation and keto–enol tautomerism (at the active methylene between the two
carbonyls) change its conjugation, giving a bathochromic (red) shift – the colour turns orange to red.
Procedure: about 1 g turmeric in 100 mL warm water (filter); equal volumes of indicator in each tube; add
a few drops of each substance (lemon juice, vinegar, soda water, Sprite, baking-soda solution, soap,
detergent, tap water, soda ash); compare colours on a white background; arrange from acidic to alkaline.
Validation: test an unknown and compare with standard pH paper.
Limitation: turmeric is yellow over the whole acidic–neutral range, so it separates acids from bases well
but can’t rank acids against each other; it’s an approximate scale.
Precautions: clean glassware (contamination), equal volumes of indicator, safe disposal.
