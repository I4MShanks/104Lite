Fundamentals & Signal Calibration Curve
Calibration is foundation of any
quantitative analysis
1. Take known standards and
corresponding observed data
2. Plot a CALIBRATION CURVE
3. Range is important
Signal Response Formula
Analytical signal response S = kC + Sb
converts analyte concentration (C) into
measurable electrical output with
baseline intercept Sb.
1. ANALYTICAL
FOUNDATIONS
Fundamentals & Signal Calibration Curve
Limits of Detection & Quantitation
Limit of Detection LOD = 3σ/m and
Limit of Quantitation LOQ = 10σ/m
based on standard deviation σ of blank
readings.
Meaning,
LOD is ~ 3 times the noise level
LOQ is ~10 limes the noise level

Fundamentals & Signal Calibration Curve
Classical Methods
Instrumental Methods
Gravimetric Analysis: Direct mass
determination of isolated precipitate
(e.g. BaSO4, AgCl) following
stoichiometric calculations.
Spectroscopic: Light absorption,
emission, or scattering transitions
(UV-Vis, FTIR, NMR, AAS).
Titrimetric Analysis: Equivalence
volume measurements utilizing acidbase, redox, or EDTA
complexometric indicators.
Separations & Electroanalysis:
Partitioning dynamics (HPLC, GC)
and interfacial redox potentials (CV,
Potentiometry).
1. ANALYTICAL
FOUNDATIONS
Errors & Statistical Accuracy vs Precision
Systematic Errors (Determinate):
Flaws in instrument calibration causing
directional bias; eliminated with Certified
Reference Materials (CRMs).
Random Errors (Indeterminate):
Environmental fluctuations quantified
by standard deviation and % RSD.

2. SPECTROSCOPY
Quantitative Calibration Strategies
UV-Vis Spectroscopy & Optical Architecture
Beer-Lambert Law
Absorbance A = log₁₀ (I₀ /I) = ε b c
where ε is molar absorptivity
(L·mol⁻¹·cm⁻¹), b is path length (1
cm), and c is concentration.
Chromophores & Transitions
Valence electronic transitions:
π → π* and n → π* excitation
bands (200–800 nm wavelength
range).
2. SPECTROSCOPY
FTIR & Michelson Interferometer
Hooke's Law Frequency
Interferometer & FFT
Beam splitter divides IR beam
between fixed and moving mirrors;
recorded interferogram undergoes
Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).
2. SPECTROSCOPY
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR)
Larmor Precession: Nuclear
spins (¹H, ¹³C) precess in
magnetic field B₀ at frequency
ν = γ B₀ / 2π.
Chemical Shift (δ): Measured in
ppm relative to TMS; electron
shielding constant σ shifts
resonance frequency.
2. SPECTROSCOPY
Atomic Absorption vs Plasma Emission
(AAS vs ICP-OES) for elemental analysis
Atomic Absorption (AAS)
Hollow Cathode Lamp (HCL)
elemental emission absorbed by
ground-state atomized cloud in
flame (2500 K) or graphite
furnace (3000 K, pg sensitivity).
2. SPECTROSCOPY
Atomic Absorption vs Plasma Emission
(AAS vs ICP-OES) for elemental analysis
ICP-OES Emission
High-temperature Argon plasma
torch (6000–10000 K) excites multielement photon emission measured
simultaneously down to ppb levels.

Mass Spectrometry Architecture
Ionization Sources
Hard EI (70 eV), Fragment finger
printing Vs. Soft ESI and MALDI
(Intact protonated [M+H]+ molecular
ions)
Mass Analyzers
Quadrupole RF Filters, Time of Flight

Separation Science & Chromatography
3. SEPARATION
SCIENCE
Gas Chromatography
Capillary Columns & Detectors
Fused silica capillary coated with
polysiloxanes for liquid samples
Molecular sieve or packed
columns for gas samples.
Flame Ionization (FID) or Electron
Capture (ECD) or Thermal
conductivity (TCD) detectors.
3. SEPARATION
SCIENCE
High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)
Reversed-Phase HPLC (RP-HPLC)
Hydrophobic non-polar stationary
phase (C18 silica) combined with
polar aqueous-organic gradient
elution (water/acetonitrile).
Column Resolution Formula
Resolution
Rs = 2(tr2 - tr1)/(w1+w2) ≥ 1.5
guarantees complete baseline
separation between adjacent
chromatographic peaks.
3. SEPARATION
SCIENCE
Ion Chromatography & Size Exclusion (SEC)
Ion Chromatography (IC)
Functionalized resin exchange
columns separate inorganic
anions/cations; membrane suppressor
eliminates eluent ions for
conductometric detection.
Size Exclusion (SEC/GPC)
Porous gel bead matrix fractionates
polymers based on hydrodynamic
radius: Ve = V0 + Ksec.Vi. Larger
molecules elute first.
3. SEPARATION
SCIENCE
Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC / HPTLC)
Retention Factor Calculation
Retention Factor Rf = danalyte / dsolvent
measured on silica gel 60 F₂₅₄ plates
(optimal range 0.2 to 0.8).
High-Performance TLC (HPTLC)
Automated spray applicators,
chamber humidity controls, and
UV/Fluorescence densitometry
scanners for quantitative analysis.
Thermal & Surface Analytical Methods
5. THERMAL & SURFACE
Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA)
Mass Loss Thermogram
Microbalance continuously records
sample weight loss (%) as a function
of controlled temperature ramp
under N₂ or air atmosphere.
Derivative TGA (DTG)
First derivative curve dm/dT
resolves overlapping decomposition
steps into distinct kinetic inflection
peaks.
5. THERMAL & SURFACE
Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC)
Thermal Transitions
Measures differential heat flow to
identify Glass Transition (Tg),
Crystallization (Tc exothermic), and
Melting (Tm endothermic).
Enthalpy Integration
Area under DSC transition peak
yields transition enthalpy
ΔH = ∫ (dq/dt) dt and percentage
crystallinity.
5. THERMAL & SURFACE
Surface & Microscopic Imaging (SEM, TEM, AFM)
SEM vs TEM Resolution
SEM measures secondary/backscattered
electrons for 3D surface morphology (1 nm).
TEM transmits high-energy electrons (200
kV) for sub-nanometer lattice structure.
Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)
Probes interatomic van der Waals forces
using a nanoscale cantilever tip for nondestructive 3D topography.
5. THERMAL & SURFACE
Surface Area and Surface property
BET Surface analysis
Adsoprtion and Desorption isotherms
Types depending upon nature of surface
Other information available are pore size
distribution, pore volume, Wall Thickness .
Chemisorption
Looks in to the acidity and basicity
properties for solids
Surface coverage and distribution of guest
species may also be determined.
Summary & Analytical Strategy
Selecting the Optimal Measurement Technique
Method selection depends on sample physical state, concentration range (ppm/ppb/ppt), matrix complexity, required
precision (%RSD), and non-destructive requirements.
