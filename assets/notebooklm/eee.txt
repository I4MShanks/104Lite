Definitions
 The measurement of any quantity plays a very important role not only in science but in all branches of
engineering, medicine, and in almost all human day-to-day activities
 The measurement of a given quantity is the act of a quantitative comparison between a predefined standard
and an unknown quantity to be measured
 The unknown quantity to be measured by an instrument/sensor is called measurand
 Measurands can be physical, chemical or biological quantities
Contd..
Physical Measurands:
• Length/Distance – meters, millimeters, kilometers
• Mass/Weight – kilograms, grams
• Time – seconds, minutes, hours
• Temperature – Celsius, Kelvin, Fahrenheit
• Electric quantities – voltage (V), current (A), resistance (Ω)
• Mechanical properties – force (N), pressure (Pa), torque
Chemical Measurands:
• Concentration – mass concentration (mg/dL), substance concentration (mmol/L)
• Activity concentration – e.g., enzyme activity in plasma
• pH – hydrogen ion concentration
• Composition ratios, viscosity and density
Contd..
Biological/Clinical Measurands:
• Biomarker levels – glucose concentration in serum and blood
• Hormone levels – thyroid hormone concentration in blood
• Cell counts – red blood cell count, white blood cell count
• Enzyme activity – alkaline phosphatase activity concentration
 The major problem with any measuring instrument is the error. Hence, it is necessary to select the
appropriate measuring instrument and measurement procedure that minimizes the error.
 Error is defined as the difference between the actual (true) value and the measured value of an instrument.
 Calibration is the process of making an adjustment or marking a scale so that the readings of an
instrument/sensor agree with the accepted certified standard.

Methods of measurement
 Direct method:
The value of a quantity is obtained directly by comparing the unknown with the standard.
Direct methods are common for the measurement of physical quantities such as length, mass, and time. It
involves no mathematical calculations to arrive at the results, for example, the measurement of length by a
graduated scale.
 Indirect method:
In this method, several other quantities (to which the quantity to be measured is linked) are measured
directly and then the unknown quantity value is determined by a mathematical relationship.
For example, the measurement of density by measuring mass and geometrical dimensions.

Primary sensing element
The first element in any measuring system (measuring instrument) is the primary sensing element: this gives an
output that is a function of the measurand.

Variable conversion element
Variable conversion elements are needed when a primary sensing element output is in an inconvenient form
and must be converted to a more convenient one.
Contd..
For instance, the force-measuring strain gauge has an output in the form of a varying resistance. Because the
resistance change cannot be measured easily, it is converted to a change in voltage by a bridge circuit, which is
a typical example of a variable conversion element.
Strain gauge

Variable manipulation element
A variable manipulation element (signal processing) exists to improve the quality of the output of a
measurement system in some way.
Contd..
For example, thermocouples have a typical output of only a few millivolts. Other types of signal processing
elements are those that filter out induced noise and remove mean levels, etc.
Data transmission element
In addition to these three components just mentioned, some measurement systems have one or two other
components: first, to transmit the signal to some remote point, and second, to display or record the signal if it is
not fed automatically into a feedback control system.
Contd..
The signal transmission element has traditionally consisted of single or multicore cable, which is often screened
to minimize signal corruption by induced electrical noise.
Wireless acceleration transmission
Contd..
 In some cases, the final element is omitted altogether because the measurement is used as part of an

 In other cases, this element in the measurement system takes the form of either a signal presentation unit or
a signal-recording unit.
Static characteristics
 Accuracy is defined as the degree of closeness with which an instrument reading approaches the true value
of the quantity being measured. It determines the closeness to the true value of instrument reading.
 Accuracy is expressed as a percentage of full-scale reading in terms of inaccuracy or in terms of error value.
 For example, the accuracy of an instrument is specified by ±5% for the range of 0 to 200ºC in the
temperature scale means, the reading might be within + or -10ºC of the true reading.
Precision is the degree of repeatability of a series of measurements.
 It is the closeness of measured values among themselves
 Measured values may be far from the true value (not accurate)
 So a precision instrument need not always be accurate, but an accurate instrument will always be precise

 Sensitivity is defined as the ratio of change in the output signal (response) to the change in the input signal
(measurand). It is the relationship indicating how much the output changes when the input changes.
 If the sensitivity is constant then the system is said to be a linear system. If the sensitivity is variable, then
the system is said to be a non-linear system.
Contd..
 Linearity of the instrument refers to the output is linearly or directly proportional to input over the entire
range of instrument. The degree of linear (straight line) relationship between the output and input is called as
linearity of an instrument.
Nonlinearity: The maximum difference or deviation of the
input-output curve from the specified idealized straight line
Contd..
 Hysteresis is a non-coincidence between the loading and unloading curves in the input-output characteristics.
The hysteresis effect shows up in any physical, chemical, or electrical phenomenon.
 When input increases, output also increases and a calibration
curve can be drawn. If input decreases from maximum value
and output also decreases but does not follow the same
curve, then there is a residual output when input is zero.
 This phenomenon is called Hysteresis. The difference
between increasing and decreasing change of output values
with respect to input is known as hysteresis error.
Contd..
 If the input to an instrument is increased gradually from zero, the input will have to reach a certain minimum
level before the change in the instrument output reading is of a large enough magnitude to be detectable. This
minimum level of input is known as the threshold of the instrument.
 The minimum magnitude change in the input measured quantity that produces an observable change in the
instrument output is called resolution.
Contd..
 Dead zone or dead band is defined as the largest change of input quantity for which there is no output the
instrument due the factors such as friction, backlash and hysteresis within the system. (The region up to
which the instrument does not respond to an input change is called dead zone).
 Dead time is the time required by an instrument to begin to respond to a change in input quantity.
 Range can be defined as the measure of the instrument between the lowest and highest readings it can
measure. A thermometer has a scale from −40°C to 100°C. Thus the range varies from −40°C to 100°C.
 Repeatability describes the closeness of output readings when the same input is applied repetitively over a
short period of time, with the same measurement conditions, same instrument and observer, same location,
and same conditions of use maintained throughout.
 Reproducibility describes the closeness of output readings for the same input when there are changes in the
method of measurement, observer, measuring instrument, location, conditions of use, and time of
measurement.
Contd..
Drift is an undesirable change in the instrument input-output characteristics over a period of time that is
unrelated to change in its input.

LVDT
The term LVDT stands for the Linear Variable Differential Transformer.
It is the most widely used inductive transducer that converts the linear motion into an electrical signal.
The output across secondary of this transformer is the differential thus it is called so.
We use LVDT in the applications where displacements to be measured that are ranging from a fraction
of mm to few cm.
The LVDT can also act as a secondary sensor. E.g. the Bourbon tube which acts as a primary transducer
and it converts pressure into linear displacement and then LVDT coverts this displacement into an
electrical signal.
LVDT
LVDT consists of a primary winding P and two secondary windings S1 and S2 wound on a cylindrical
former (which is hollow in nature and contains the core).
Both the secondary windings have an equal number of turns, and we place them on either side of
primary winding
The primary winding is connected to an AC source which produces a flux in the air gap and voltages
are induced in both the secondary windings.
A movable soft iron core is placed inside the cylindrical former and the displacement to be measured is
connected to the iron core.
The iron core is generally of high permeability which helps in reducing harmonics and also to obtain
high sensitivity.
Contd..

Contd..
Contd..
 The primary winding is energized by an a.c. voltage of
amplitude VP and frequency f Hz; the two secondary coils
are connected in series opposition so that the output voltage
is the difference (V1−V2) of the voltages induced in the
secondary coils
V1−V2 =VOUT sin(2π f t + φ)
 With the core in the former, V1 and V2 change with core
position x, causing amplitude VOUT and phase φ to change.
Contd..
Here the secondary coils are equally coupled to the primary and V1 and V2 have
approximately equal amplitudes; V1−V2 has therefore minimum amplitude (ideally
zero) and this position is termed the null point.

secondary coil is weakly coupled to the primary; V1 has greater amplitude than V2 and
V1 − V2 is in phase with the primary coil voltage.

Advantages
High Range – The LVDTs have a very high range for measurement of displacement. (displacements range
1.25 mm to 250 mm).
No Frictional Losses – As the core moves inside a hollow former so, there is no loss of displacement input as
frictional loss so, it makes LVDT as very accurate device.
High Input and High Sensitivity – The output of LVDT is so high that it doesn’t need any amplification.
Low Hysteresis – LVDTs show a low hysteresis and hence repeatability is excellent under all conditions
Low Power Consumption – The power is about 1W which is very small as compared to other transducers.
Direct Conversion to Electrical Signals – They convert the linear displacement to electrical voltage which
are easy to process.

LAB MANUAL SECTION FOR THIS LECTURE'S EXPERIMENT:

5. LVDT: sensitivity of a displacement transducer (EEE)
Aim: study the measurement protocol and find the sensitivity of an LVDT.
1. Calibrate the CRO: 1 V/div and 1 ms/div; connect the Y-input to the CAL terminal – you should see 1 V
amplitude at 1 kHz.
2. Wire the LVDT; feed 1 kHz, 10 V from the signal generator to the primary.
3. Adjust the screw gauge until the CRO shows a straight horizontal line – zero output, the null point.
Note the screw-gauge reading there.
4. Move the cantilever plate (and core) up and down with the screw gauge; record the displacement from
null and the CRO output each time.
5. Plot output vs displacement; slope = ΔY/ΔX = sensitivity (e.g. mV/mm). The graph is linear on both
sides of null; in the CRO the phase flips by 180° when you cross null.
