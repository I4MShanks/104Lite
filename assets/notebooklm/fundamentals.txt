Basic Concepts of Measurements
Physical Quantity
 Physical quantity is a property of a material or system
that can be measured
 Base quantities are distinct quantities in nature that
can’t be defined in terms of other quantities
 Length, Time, Mass
 Derived quantities can be defined in terms of the
base quantities
 Density, Volume
Base and Derived Quantities
 The seven base quantities of the International System
of Quantities (ISQ)
 Length, Mass, Time, Temperature
 Electric current, Amount of substance
 Luminous intensity
 Derived physical quantities (Force, speed, etc.) can be
described by mathematically combining these seven
base quantities.
Measurement and its Unit
 Measurement is process of determining the
value of an unknown physical quantity with
respect to a pre-defined standard
 Physical quantity can be expressed as the
combination of a numerical value and unit
 Unit of measurement is a definite magnitude of
a quantity, defined and adopted by convention
System of units
 System of units varies from country to country
 CGS system of units
 FPS system of units
 MKS system of units
 International System of Units (SI)
 Base SI units
 Metre, Kilogram, Second, Kelvin, Ampere, Candela and Mole
 All other SI units can be derived from these base units.
Readability and Least count
 Readability of instrument
Least Count = 1 mm
 Indicates the closeness with which the
scale of the instrument may be read.
 Least count of instrument
 Smallest difference between two
indications that can be detected on the
instrument scale.
 Readability and least count are
dependent on scale length, spacing of
graduations, etc.
Least Count = 0.5 mm
Hysteresis
 Instrument is said to exhibit
hysteresis when there is a difference
in readings depending on whether the
value of the measured quantity is
approached from above or below
 Reason for Hysteresis
 Mechanical friction
 Elastic deformation
 Thermal effects
Accuracy and Precision
 Accuracy of an instrument indicates the deviation of the
reading from a known input.
 Expressed as a percentage of full-scale reading or an absolute
value
 Precision of an instrument indicates its ability to reproduce
a certain reading with a given accuracy
 Accuracy can be improved up to but not beyond the
precision of the instrument by calibration.

Calibration
 Calibration
 Checking the instrument against a

 Reduce errors in accuracy
 Establishes the accuracy of the

Analysis of Experimental Data
Introduction
 Statistics/statistical analysis is a mathematical tool for
quantitative analysis of measurement data and
extracting useful information from data.
 Summarize the observations by estimating the
average (true mean) and the variance
(uncertainty)
 Propagate the measurement error through a
mathematical model to estimate the error in the
derived quantity.
Error and Uncertainty
 Error = Measured value– true value. It is often expressed
in percentage as: (Measured value– true value)/ True
value × 100
 Uncertainty in a stated measurement is the interval of
confidence around the measured value such that the
measured value is certain not to lie outside this stated
interval.
 Uncertainty interval is usually assigned in the following
formats: L = 1.00 ± 0.01 m or 1.00 m ± 1%. Uncertainties
may also be stated along with a probability.
Types of Errors
 Errors in instrument readings may be
classified in to three categories:
 Gross errors/Blunder
 Systematic/Fixed errors
 Random Errors
 Gross errors arise due to
human mistakes
 Reading the instrument value
before it reaches steady state
 Parallax error in reading on an
analogue scale
 Careful reading and recording
of data can reduce the gross
error
